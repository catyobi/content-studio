# Automated video editing: technical feasibility

**Recommendation: proceed to a local proof of concept only after approval.** A native FFmpeg render produced a 27-second, 1080 × 1920 MP4 from five mixed-orientation fixtures in a median of 11.20 seconds on one CPU-only test machine. The corrected benchmark verified dissolve timing and the output encoding settings; it did not validate automatic edit quality or arbitrary phone footage. Use a versioned timeline specification and a replaceable FFmpeg worker; keep the future mobile web UI separate from export.

## What the engine must do

- Take five user-owned original clips, automatically select a range from each, and arrange the ranges into a coherent cut.
- Export a 25–30 second vertical MP4 at exactly 1080 × 1920. Use H.264, 30 fps, square pixels and `yuv420p` as the initial compatibility profile; test playback on phones rather than assuming that the profile is sufficient.
- Apply a configurable logo, palette and typography, and support hard cuts and simple dissolves.
- Handle landscape, portrait and square footage, including files whose displayed orientation comes from metadata. Do not stretch shots or require that users reformat their originals.
- Leave original files intact. The export re-encodes selected source frames and composites only edits and supplied brand assets; no generated scenes, replacement footage, or outpainting.

## Technology comparison

Complexity and performance below are relative to this five-clip workflow. Only native FFmpeg was timed; the other assessments are based on their architecture and published documentation, not comparative measurements.

No service purchase or deployed infrastructure is needed for a local evaluation. A hosted editing API is not recommended: it adds per-render costs, upload/privacy dependencies and a proprietary timeline contract. If hosting is approved later, first estimate **compute per successful export ≈ billed worker seconds × hourly rate / 3,600**, with separate accounting for failed/retried jobs, queue idle time, proxy generation, retained originals, exports and egress. At an illustrative $0.10 per worker-hour and 11.20 billed seconds, compute alone would be about $0.00031 per render; this is not a price quote or a production estimate. Real phone footage and concurrency can materially change it.

### FFmpeg measurement: one graph, no deployed infrastructure

Ubuntu 22.04.5 LTS (Linux x86-64), Intel Xeon virtual processor with 8 logical CPUs visible, 31 GiB RAM visible, no GPU acceleration. The corrected benchmark used FFmpeg `7.0.2-static` with GPL-enabled `libx264`; the local FFmpeg 4.4.2 build with FreeType prepared fixtures and static branding assets. GNU `time` measured three consecutive renders with identical local inputs, one job at a time. This version is a test reference, not a recommendation to ship an old binary.

| Run | Wall time | CPU time (user + system) | Peak RSS | Output |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 11.57 s | 44.61 s | 1,453,144 KiB | 27.000 s, 810 frames |
| 2 | 11.20 s | 45.77 s | 1,446,328 KiB | 27.000 s, 810 frames |
| 3 | 10.50 s | 42.83 s | 1,440,788 KiB | 27.000 s, 810 frames |

**What the original measurement missed.** The FFmpeg 4.4.2 export reported 810 frames and 27.000 seconds, even with evenly spaced decoded timestamps. A diagnostic rerender with `showinfo` just before encoding counted only 807 graph output frames: PTS 179, 338 and 495 were absent on the 30 fps clock. The encoder command reported `dup=3 drop=0` when filling the gaps. `freezedetect` flagged 5.96667–6.06667 seconds near the first handoff; exact hashes alone did not flag it. The nominal arithmetic, `5 × 6 − 4 × 0.75 = 27` seconds, was right in continuous time, but each 0.75-second dissolve spans 22.5 frames. At 30 fps, the offsets 5.25, 10.50, 15.75 and 21.00 seconds round to frames 158, 315, 473 and 630. Changing only to integral offsets and 22/23-frame durations on 4.4.2 still gave the same three timestamp gaps, so rounding was not sufficient to explain or fix them.

The original probe **did** check `yuv420p`. A fuller probe of the original export found H.264 High@4.0, SAR 1:1, DAR 9:16 and `moov` before `mdat`; color range, matrix, primaries and transfer were unspecified. In contrast, all corrected exports reported H.264 High@4.0, 1080 × 1920, `yuv420p` (8-bit 4:2:0), 30 fps, SAR 1:1, DAR 9:16, TV/limited range and BT.709 matrix, primaries and transfer. Top-level boxes were `ftyp`, `moov`, `free`, `mdat`. Each file was 13,144,305 bytes.

To reproduce the workload:

1. Make five 7-second `testsrc2` inputs at 1920 × 1080 @ 30, 1080 × 1920 @ 24, 1080 × 1080 @ 25, 1280 × 720 @ 60, and 720 × 1280 @ 30. For each, use `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "testsrc2=s=WIDTHxHEIGHT:r=FPS:d=7" -vf "drawbox=x=0:y=0:w=iw:h=ih/8:color=COLOR:t=fill" -c:v libx264 -threads 2 -preset ultrafast -crf 20 -color_range tv -colorspace smpte170m -color_trc bt709 -color_primaries bt709 input.mp4`; assign red, lime, blue, yellow and magenta stripes in order to identify the source in each frame. Prepare a 168 × 96 RGBA logo and a 460 × 100 RGBA title PNG; rasterize “Content Studio” in DejaVu Sans at 54 px, gold `#F9C74F` on translucent navy `#102A43`. The fixture patterns and sample branding are for this test only, not generated footage in the proposed engine.
2. Probe each source for enough media to cover its requested trim. In `-filter_complex`, trim each source with `trim=start=0.5:duration=6,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS,fps=30,settb=1/30`. Then use `scale=1080:1920:force_original_aspect_ratio=increase:force_divisible_by=2:in_color_matrix=bt601:out_color_matrix=bt709:in_range=tv:out_range=tv,crop=1080:1920,setsar=1,format=yuv444p,setparams=range=limited:color_primaries=bt709:color_trc=bt709:colorspace=bt709`. In an untimed diagnostic pass, send each normalized branch through `showinfo` to null and verify 180 frames at PTS 0–179, with matching geometry, SAR, rate, time base, color and pixel format. This converts the fixture matrix before tagging it; relabeling unknown phone footage as BT.709 is not a normalization strategy.
3. Calculate each dissolve's `offset = accumulated_frames − overlap_frames`; update `accumulated_frames = offset + next_clip_frames`. Use verified trim lengths and overlaps of 22, 23, 22 and 23 frames to start at 158, 315, 473 and 630, with the first fully incoming frames at 180, 338, 495 and 653. Total: `5 × 180 − (22 + 23 + 22 + 23) = 810` frames. Chain four `xfade=transition=fade` filters with those frame counts expressed as seconds (divide each value by 30; pass at least six decimal places). Convert the result to `yuv420p`, convert the two PNGs to BT.709 limited `yuva420p`, and overlay the logo at `x=W-w-72:y=112` and title at `x=72:y=H-h-140`.
4. Time only the combined render: `-threads 2` before each clip input, `-filter_complex_threads 2`, then map the final output with `-an -fps_mode passthrough -c:v libx264 -threads 4 -preset veryfast -crf 22 -profile:v high -level:v 4.0 -pix_fmt yuv420p -color_range tv -colorspace bt709 -color_trc bt709 -color_primaries bt709 -movflags +faststart`. Use passthrough timing so the output step cannot silently fill gaps. Use `ffprobe -show_streams -show_format` to assert the video properties above and parse top-level MP4 boxes to assert `moov` precedes the first `mdat`. With `ffprobe -select_streams v:0 -show_frames -show_entries frame=best_effort_timestamp`, convert timestamps using the stream time base; require exactly 810 frames in presentation order at `n/30`, starting at zero, and 27.000 seconds total.
5. Scan the decoded output with `blackdetect=d=0.066:pix_th=0.10:pic_th=0.98,freezedetect=n=-60dB:d=0.10` and compare adjacent decoded `framemd5` hashes. Check clip identity and fade timing independently of these heuristics: sample the RGB mean of the 16 × 16 stripe patch at `(144, 64)` on **every** frame. Compare with the expected steady source color or the frame-weighted blend of the two interior swatches from the edit timeline (maximum error 12 per 8-bit RGB channel for this fixture). Inspect a contact sheet for each join: the frame before, a midpoint, and the first fully incoming frame. Run a negative control with only the first offset delayed by nine frames (158 → 167); keep the expected timeline and later offsets unchanged.

**Results.** All three corrected renders had continuous PTS, no detected black/frozen spans or exact adjacent duplicates, and zero source/blend mismatches (max channel error 4.70). Visual spot checks confirmed outgoing, blended and incoming frames at all four joins with the logo and title present. The late-offset control still passed duration, frame count, PTS, black/freeze, exact-duplicate and format checks, but the source/blend check rejected **20 frames**, starting at frame 160 (max channel error 105.32). Generic gap detectors cannot substitute for checking what was on screen when.

Timing includes decode, filter, encode and local file output; it excludes fixture and overlay creation, diagnostic passes, validation, uploads and scheduling. The rerun changed both the build and fixture/overlay workload, so its timing is not a version-to-version speed comparison. This was a fixed-trim, center-crop, silent synthetic test. It does not establish real-footage visual quality, audio sync, phone playback, HDR/HEVC, rotation-metadata handling, concurrency or automatic shot selection, and the encoding fields alone do not prove decoder compatibility.

## Recommended architecture

**Originals → media probe → edit planner → validated timeline → FFmpeg worker → verified MP4.** Run the entire pipeline locally with files and CLI tools. No API, cloud account or external model is required by the design.

1. **Ingest and probe.** Treat the five originals as read-only assets. Use `ffprobe` JSON to record duration, streams, rotation/display matrix, pixel and sample aspect, timestamps, codec and color information. Start with explicitly supported input profiles and return per-clip errors for corrupt, unsupported or over-budget media. Keep media data, derived intermediates and render outputs separate.
2. **Plan a draft deterministically.** Start with usable ranges that avoid black starts/ends and obvious interruptions; use cheap brightness and coarse motion signals as hints, not a claim of semantic understanding. Allocate a balanced share of the target duration across all five clips, redistributing unused frames from short clips. Keep input order as the fallback; where available, use a simple opening/closing signal or manual role to place an opener and closer, leaving the intervening order stable. Save the choices so users can override in/out, order or framing without rescanning media. Calculate target frames as the sum of selected lengths minus overlaps; shorten dissolves or use cuts when handles are too short. If there is insufficient usable footage even with cuts, report it instead of repeating or inventing footage.
3. **Use an engine-neutral, versioned timeline.** Validate asset IDs, source ranges, ordered segments, rational source timestamps and integer output frame positions, fit/focal point, transition type and frame count, audio policy, canvas profile and brand settings. Reference actual logo/font assets and their versions, rather than host font names or FFmpeg expressions. Give jobs a spec version, immutable asset identifiers and renderer build information so renders can be replayed on another machine. Keep ordering/range selection out of the filtergraph compiler.
4. **Normalize and composite in a worker.** Pin a maintained FFmpeg build; normalize orientation exactly once (honor the display matrix, then do not carry it into the export), normalize timestamps, frame rate, time base, aspect and color pipeline before joins. Display the full frame by default using contain with a solid brand-color pad; allow cover with a focal point or optional blurred padding derived from the same source. Use concatenation for cuts and `xfade` between normalized streams for dissolves. Add an alpha PNG logo and basic text with a pinned font via `drawtext`; if complex text shaping is needed, render text-only overlays with a tested layout engine. Validate color, placement and safe margins. If retaining clip audio, trim from the same clock, normalize channel layout and sample rate, insert silence for missing streams and use `acrossfade` for dissolves. Start with a simple explicit audio choice rather than assuming audio can be discarded.
5. **Package and check.** Export MP4 with fast-start metadata; validate actual stream properties, continuous presentation timestamps and layout of the MP4 boxes. Test join content against the timeline with fixture-based checks instead of using duration and frame count as a proxy. Keep the graph compiler and local worker small; use a process argument list, bounded resource use, and local trusted paths instead of evaluating user-supplied filter strings or URLs. A future job queue/storage adapter can change without changing timeline semantics; nothing in the spec requires a vendor or hosted renderer.
6. **Preview on mobile, render off device.** Use `<video playsinline>` with local compatible clips or low-resolution H.264 proxies as bandwidth allows. Drive trim handles, reorder controls, fit and overlays from the same spec; exact dissolve timing and typography should be checked via a rendered proxy/reference when browser preview differs. Plan for resumable uploads and asynchronous export status when the web app is approved. Treat WebCodecs as a progressive enhancement, not an availability requirement.

## Technical risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation / what to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Technically valid but unconvincing edits | Evaluate on consented real clips with the owner; review openings, abrupt cuts, clipped speech and weak framing. Keep deterministic fallbacks, explain which ranges were picked and support overrides. |
| Portrait metadata, VFR, anamorphic or HDR/HEVC inputs | Probe before editing; normalize rotation, display aspect and timestamps; define and test a deliberate SDR tone-map path or return a clear unsupported-media error. Verify color and time sync against reference clips, not only file metadata. |
| Cropping people, obscuring text or differing preview/export glyphs | Use contain by default, optional focal points and visible safe areas, consistent asset versions, and test alpha, contrast and glyph coverage; do not silently fall back to a different font. |
| A/V drift, audible cuts and mixed/missing tracks | Anchor audio and video to the same timeline; test padding, sample-accurate trimming, crossfades, and whether loudness handling is needed. |
| Performance spikes and large uploads | Cap source duration, size and decoded pixels; bound worker concurrency, scratch and time; benchmark heavier inputs and report upload/proxy latency independently. |
| User footage and untrusted media | Patch and isolate the worker; restrict input paths and network access, bound logs and runtime, and define retention/deletion for originals, proxies and exports before any shared service. |
| License or codec obligations | Before distributing binaries, WASM or container images, review FFmpeg build flags, applicable GPL/LGPL redistribution obligations, any encoder/font/brand-asset licenses and H.264/AAC patent questions separately. A CLI boundary makes the component replaceable; it is not a legal conclusion. |

## Proposed proof of concept (approval required)

1. Assemble a consented local fixture set: five mixed-aspect shots per job, including a rotated phone recording, VFR, mixed/missing audio, off-center subject, short source, and an invalid file. Include a 4K/HEVC or HDR stress case to qualify or explicitly reject it. Establish the supported input envelope before comparing timings.
2. Define the timeline schema and a simple deterministic planner. Test that every viable job uses all five clips, handles insufficient footage with a useful error, stays between 25 and 30 seconds after overlap accounting, and produces the same timeline on repeated runs with identical inputs. Review at least three real five-clip sets with the owner for trim, narrative order and framing; record why edits need changes.
3. Build only a local CLI prototype using a pinned worker. Test contain/cover, rotation, no stretching, and correct logo placement, colors and fonts for two distinct brand presets. Compare original checksums before and after; account for all exported footage with the timeline. Require these acceptance checks:
   - **Dissolves and cuts:** Each normalized branch must match its configured trim length on the common frame clock, with sufficient frames for its overlaps. Export exactly the planned frame count, with continuous `n/30` presentation timestamps and no output frame-rate repair. On marked synthetic fixtures, require zero incorrect source/blend frames for cuts and all four dissolves; require the deliberately late offset to fail that check even if the count and duration pass. For real clips, log black/freeze spans and duplicate-frame candidates around each join, and inspect before/mid/after frames against the planned source ranges. Require zero unexplained black spans, gaps or holds; distinguish intentionally dark/static footage and expected frame repeats during frame-rate conversion from export defects.
   - **Video compatibility:** For every accepted export require a 25–30 second MP4, exactly 1080 × 1920 at constant 30 fps, H.264 High at level 4.0, `yuv420p`, SAR 1:1, DAR 9:16, limited/BT.709 SDR signaling and `moov` before `mdat`. Use reference frames to check that color values were actually converted and not merely relabeled. Require correctly oriented playback, seeking and full playback on iOS Safari and Android Chrome, not just a passing probe.
   - **Audio if retained:** With a marked flash/click fixture, require audio/video presentation durations and sync at the start, each join and the end to agree within one video frame (1/30 s), accounting for AAC priming. Check that missing input streams produce the planned silence and that dissolves introduce no unintended audible gaps.
4. Measure wall time, peak memory, output size and failure modes for repeated single jobs and two concurrent jobs on declared hardware. Use a provisional local target of at most 60 seconds and 2 GiB peak RSS per single render on similar 8-logical-CPU hardware for the supported fixture envelope, not as a production SLA. Play the export on iOS Safari and Android Chrome; test a low-resolution preview against the same timeline and note any parity gaps. Compare Remotion only if FFmpeg's branding/template constraints fail the owner's review and the license is viable.

**Open decisions:** Which orientation treatment should users see by default? Should original speech be preserved, muted or mixed? What makes a good opener/closer for the intended clips? Which phone formats and source file limits must the first release accept? What brand safe areas and font rights are required? What export wait time and retention policy are acceptable? Implementation pauses here pending approval.
